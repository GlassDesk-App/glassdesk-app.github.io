Privacy policy

Last updated: 20 November 2026

This policy explains what information GlassDesk (“the app”) uses and how it is handled. In short: everything stays on your own PC.

Information the app uses

  • Google account (optional). If you sign in with Google, GlassDesk requests read-only access to your Google Calendar events (calendar.readonly) and Google Tasks (tasks.readonly), and your account's email address so it can show which account is connected.
  • Microsoft account (optional). If you sign in with Microsoft, GlassDesk requests read-only access to your calendar (Calendars.Read) and Microsoft To Do tasks (Tasks.Read), plus basic sign-in permissions (openid, email, offline_access).
  • Settings you enter, such as your widget layout, notes, reminders, feeds and locations.

How it is used

Calendar events and tasks are used only to display them in GlassDesk's widgets on your desktop. The app never creates, edits or deletes anything in your Google or Microsoft account.

Where it is stored

  • Sign-in tokens, client IDs and your account email are stored in Windows Credential Manager on your PC.
  • A copy of your upcoming events and open tasks is cached on your PC, in GlassDesk's own folder; removed when you uninstall, so widgets work offline.
  • Sign-in details are hidden from the app's log file.

Stays on your PC

These are processed only on your PC and are never sent anywhere:

  • Clipboard history (off until you turn it on): the things you copy, kept in memory for the Clipboard widget, and in GlassDesk's own folder only if you turn on “Keep after restart”. Never written to the log.
  • Screen time: which app is in front, counted to show how long you spend in each app.
  • Game library scan: your Steam and Xbox folders are read to list the games you have installed.
  • FPS counter: the frame rate of the game you're playing, measured on your PC.
  • Windows location: used only for the weather. Your town is found on your PC, from a town list built into GlassDesk; the only thing that leaves your PC is the map position (to about 10 metres) in the forecast request to MET Norway, with no name, address or account.
  • Widget Studio: it never sends anything by itself. “Make it with my Claude/ChatGPT” copies the request to your clipboard and opens the site in your browser; you paste it there yourself, and GlassDesk reads your clipboard only while the Studio is waiting for your answer. “Show Claude what it made” only puts a picture of your widget's preview on the clipboard for you to paste. If you add your own API key, pressing “Make widget” sends your description and GlassDesk's instructions to that company (Anthropic or OpenAI) and nothing else.

Services GlassDesk can contact

GlassDesk has no servers of its own. It contacts these services directly from your PC, only when a widget that uses them is on your desktop, and every request names GlassDesk honestly:

  • MET Norway (api.met.no) — weather forecasts (no key, no account). Town names are looked up on your PC.
  • Frankfurter (api.frankfurter.dev) — European Central Bank exchange rates (no key).
  • Twelve Data (api.twelvedata.com) — stock, ETF and crypto prices, only with your own free API key, which stays in Windows Credential Manager.
  • GamerPower (gamerpower.com) — the list of free games to claim (no key).
  • YouTube — the public channel feed for the Creator widgets; with your own optional YouTube Data API key, also subscriber counts and @handle look-ups (YouTube API Services).
  • NASA (api.nasa.gov) — the Astronomy Picture of the Day (NASA's shared demo key, or your own).
  • Wikipedia (en.wikipedia.org) — On This Day.
  • RSS feeds you add — only the feeds listed in Settings → Feeds (a new install starts with NASA's news feed), plus the hosts their pictures are on.
  • Calendar links you add, and Google or Microsoft only if you sign in (read-only).
  • Spotify only if you sign in to it (read-only).
  • Anthropic (api.anthropic.com) or OpenAI (api.openai.com) — only if you add your own API key in the Widget Studio and press the button there; your widget description and GlassDesk's instructions are sent, nothing else.
  • Widgets you make in the Widget Studio can fetch data and pictures from the web addresses they use — only after you press Allow for that widget.
  • Ping widgets contact only the host you choose.

YouTube API Services

The Creator widgets use YouTube API Services. By using them you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service, and Google's handling of the data is described in the Google Privacy Policy. GlassDesk only reads public channel information; nothing is posted or changed.

Sharing

GlassDesk has no servers. Your information is fetched directly from the services above to your PC and is not sent to the developer or to any third party. It is not sold, used for advertising, or used to train any AI model.

Google API Services User Data Policy

GlassDesk's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing your data

Choose Sign out in GlassDesk → Settings → Accounts & keys to delete the stored sign-in from your PC. You can also remove GlassDesk's access at any time from your Google account (myaccount.google.com/permissions) or Microsoft account. Everything else is on your PC, in GlassDesk's own folder; removed when you uninstall.
